An 11-point playoff gap looks like something. It isn't. Strip out the win itself and the picture inverts: teams that lost their opener went on to win more of their remaining games — 257–223 against 223–257 — and reached more finals, 7 to 5.

The titles split exactly down the middle, three each. On a Fisher exact test the playoff difference comes out at p = 0.47: nowhere near a real effect. The 95% interval around that +11.1pp runs roughly from −11 to +34.

One sanity check settles it. Simulate a league where every manager is identical and every game is a coin flip, and a 1–0 start is still worth +19.3pp of playoff probability — purely from banking one win. The real league has managed +11.1. Week 1 has been worth less than the win itself.

02 — Week by week

Only one week all season predicted less than week 1

Run the same test on every week of the regular season: how much more often did that week's winners make the playoffs than its losers?

Week 9 is the fun one: its winners made the playoffs less often than its losers. That is noise, and so, on this evidence, is week 1. With 72 team-seasons nothing on this chart is individually significant — which is rather the point. If week 1 carried real information, six years of it should have started to show.

04 — Starts

A 1–1 start is worth about the same whichever order it arrives in — 32% after WL, 47% after LW — which is another way of saying the sequence doesn't matter, only the total. And even 0–2 isn't fatal: 2021's champion started 1–4 through five weeks, 2024's started 0–3.

The difference from redraft

This league trades futures, not players

In the redraft league next door, 64 trades over six seasons moved zero draft picks. Here, 55% of everything that changed hands was a pick — often two or three drafts out, for a player who did not exist in the league yet. Each bar below splits a franchise's incoming assets into picks and players.

The ledger

What the trades actually returned

Every asset is followed to what it became. A player is credited with the points he went on to score in his new team's starting lineup, for as long as that team kept him. A pick is resolved to the player it was eventually spent on, and credited the same way — but only to the team that actually used it. Flip a pick onward and it pays you nothing here.

Number one in the book

A backup quarterback for a spare part

+1,601 points · the largest margin in league history

andymci buys Jalen Hurts in September 2021

Hurts had eleven career starts. andymci sent Ryan Tannehill, Eric Ebron and a 2022 second-round pick, and got back Hurts and a 2022 third.

Hurts has since started 1,615.7 points' worth of fantasy football for that roster and is still there. Tannehill scored 15.0 for yaks before being dropped. Ebron never started a game for them.

It is also the whole of andymci's trading career in one line: nine trades in seven seasons, +2,325 net, and two thirds of it is this.

The picks that became somebody

Of 186 picks traded, 16 are still unspent and 57 were flipped onward before they were used. Of the 113 a team actually kept and used, 44 returned nothing at all and the median returned 15.9 points. The tail is where the league is won.

Method. Every completed trade in the league's eight Sleeper seasons (2019–2026), read from the API along with every weekly lineup, every draft and every draft-pick transfer. A traded pick is resolved through the draft's slot-to-roster map back to the player actually selected with it. Value is starting-lineup points only: a player on the bench earns his new owner nothing, which is the right test for whether a trade helped.

The runway bias. A 2019 trade has had seven seasons to pay out and a 2025 trade has had one. Nothing here is adjusted for that, so old trades dominate the top of the ledger by construction. Read the margins as what happened, not as a judgement of who is shrewd.

What a flipped pick is worth. Nothing, in this accounting. A team that trades a pick onward before the draft is credited with zero for it, even though they clearly got something back — that something is scored in the next trade instead. It is the honest way to keep the book zero-sum, but it is hard on the league's serial dealers.

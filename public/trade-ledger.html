The book of accounts

Every manager's running balance

Net points is what a manager's acquisitions scored in their starting lineup after the trade, minus what the players they gave away scored for the other side. It is zero-sum: the league's balance is exactly nil, so this is purely who took points off whom.

Number one in the book

Josh Jacobs for an ACL and a redshirt

Week 4, 2024. andymci sends Brandon Aiyuk and rookie Jonathon Brooks to YerMan, with 10 FAAB, for Josh Jacobs and Christian Watson.

+243.8 net · 5 games swung

The biggest margin in league history, by 44 points

Jacobs started all fifteen remaining weeks for andymci and scored 243.3 of his points in the lineup. Aiyuk gave YerMan three starts and 32.5 points before tearing his ACL in week 7. Brooks never played a meaningful down and finished on −0.3.

andymci went 8–6 and made the playoffs. Five of those wins came by a margin smaller than what Jacobs and Watson contributed that week, including the week-15 quarter-final. YerMan finished 6–8 and missed out.

Judged on the day, it was defensible: a round-two RB for a round-three WR. It became the best trade ever because the other side's assets stopped existing.

Leverage, not volume

The trade that swung six games was worth 41 points

Points won are not the same as games won. A "swing" is a week where a team won by less than the trade was worth to them that week — remove the trade and the result flips. On that measure the biggest deal in the book is not the biggest haul.

Method. Pulled from the Sleeper API for all six completed seasons: every completed transaction of type trade, matched against weekly matchup data so each traded player is credited only for points scored in a starting lineup. A trade's margin to one side is the points its acquisitions started for from the trade week onward, minus the points its departures started for at their new home over the same span. Playoff weeks are included.

Caveat. The counterfactual is generous: it assumes nothing replaces a departed player. In reality a bench body covers some of the loss, so every margin here is an upper bound. The ordering is sound; the sizes are not exact.

One name change. phillyson and YerMan are the same manager and the same Sleeper account (343060966679908352), renamed after the 2020 season. Both are counted here as YerMan, which is why the ledger shows 25 deals against that name. Nobody else in the league has changed handle, and the same twelve managers have played all six seasons.

Two oddities. Two recorded trades transferred no players, picks or FAAB at all and score zero. Seven were a single player for cash. No trade in league history has ever included a draft pick.

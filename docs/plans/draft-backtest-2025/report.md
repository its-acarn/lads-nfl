## How different is it, really

Pick-for-pick agreement understates how close the two drafts are, in two ways.

- **4 of 14 players appear on both rosters**: Bijan Robinson, Bucky Irving, Mark Andrews, Brandon Aiyuk.
- **0 of the divergences are the engine reordering your own picks** — it took a player you took yourself, just at a different one of your turns. No third party is involved and the roster is unaffected.
- **10 of 14 comparable divergences are within the same tier of your own board.** Where that holds, the engine is not disagreeing with you about who is better — your board says the two are interchangeable, and the engine broke the tie on roster need.

passing run mean something.

## What this cannot tell you

- **One draft, one slot.** A single sample. It can show the engine is not
  obviously broken; it cannot show it is good.
- **Opponents are semi-scripted and do not react.** Nobody adjusts to being
  sniped, which mildly favours the engine.
- **The swap assumption is convenient, not behavioural.** A manager denied a
  player would take whatever their own board said next, not necessarily the one
  Andrew took. The cascade rosters above show how much that choice moves things.
- **The ADP prior is slightly stale.** The spreadsheet was captured before
  Fantasy Football Calculator's 31 August window, so late pre-season injury news
  — Joe Mixon most visibly — had not yet moved it.
- **No points.** Whether this roster would have scored more is not answered here.
- **Nothing after draft day.** Waivers and trades are most of a fantasy season
  and none of this models them.
